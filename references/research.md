# Research and records

Use sources available to the current assistant. Check access once. Never require users to install a named tool. For each source family, log query, URL, date, candidates found, unique prospects, qualified prospects, and access state.

Choose source layers for the target: fairs, associations, registries, directories, company sites, marketplaces, public profiles, or user communities. Respect requested platforms. Deduplicate before deep research. Verify organizations on attributable business pages; verify individual leads against their own public account and the exact post/comment supporting the signal. Store profile URL and evidence URL separately. Record whether the person authored the post or only commented, the post date/age, and the usable platform route. A snippet, generic post, reaction, or follower count alone cannot establish fit. Inactive business profiles are neutral; an old individual signal lowers confidence. Do not claim broad social coverage from snippets.

Use the requested count; suggest 20 if none was given. Select source layers appropriate to the sector and access available. For manufacturers, prioritize trade fairs and wholesale directories; for local services, business listings and associations; for software, company sites and relevant industry directories. Verify each candidate independently. Keep candidates in the ledger, rank by evidence and fit, and return only qualified results. If fewer pass, identify the missing proof.

Canonical records: prospect ID; organization ID/aliases when applicable; source ID, URL, and checked date; one observation per claim with source ID; public route and source ID; decision with missing gate; draft with source ID. Keep raw excerpts out of contact export.

For organization targets, identify a relevant purchasing or decision-making role when the user needs a person. Record role, source, date, and whether the route is directly published for that person or shared by the organization. Do not infer that a founder is the buyer or manufacture an email address. For individual-user targets, qualify the person from their own public use signal and a usable account route; do not require a business email. If the needed route or identity is unverified, hold the lead.

Track discovery yield by source and segment: candidates found, unique prospects, qualified prospects and usable contact routes. Compare these counts before spending more effort; retain requested sources even when yield is low. A source listing or model score is a discovery signal, not proof of buyer fit or current intent.

Set gates to target type. Organization: identity/market, relevant buyer function, specific offer/use fit, public business route. Individual end user: public account identity, clear use-case or need signal (or user-approved audience hypothesis), usable platform route, and recency noted. A profile reaction or generic bio alone is weak evidence. Distinguish stated need from inferred audience fit. Label fit exact, adjacent, or unconfirmed. `Explore` requires identity, route, plausible relevance, and user approval; keep it visibly lower priority. Missing evidence is held, not guessed. Clear mismatch, exclusions, and duplicates are excluded.

For each candidate send AI only decisive excerpts, offer constraints, contact candidates, and source IDs. Reject a positive decision whose citation does not exist. Revisit only when source, brief, or a missing gate changes. Parallel agents own disjoint query slices and append through one import queue.
