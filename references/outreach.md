# Outreach and output

## Draft standard

Write as a credible peer, not a vendor template. Lead with the prospect's business context when a verified hook exists; otherwise use a relevant segment-level opening. Introduce sender naturally after relevance is established. Every sentence must help recognition, credibility, offer fit, or reply. If a reference message is supplied, preserve its structure, claims, order and tone unless the user approves a change.

Use the user's stated reply goal. If none is given, choose the smallest useful next step for that offer, usually a relevance or routing question; do not default to a meeting. Ask one brief clarification only when the intended next step would materially change the draft.

Personalization must connect a verified fact to the reason for outreach. Use facts such as product range, market served, role, recent business activity, hiring, event participation, or a public statement only when current and source-backed. Never manufacture a hook. Avoid generic praise, personal trivia, scraped-sounding language, guessed buyer names, invented intent, stale congratulations, or telling recipients how they were researched.

Use one primary offer angle, one proof point when approved, and one low-friction reply ask. Do not dump every feature or product. A first email should usually be short enough to read on a phone; length may expand when the user supplies an approved longer format or the offer genuinely requires detail. Read it aloud: remove marketing jargon, repetition, robotic transitions, fake familiarity, and AI patterns such as “I hope this email finds you well,” “I came across your profile,” “leverage,” “synergy,” contrast slogans, self-answered questions, and em dashes.

Email subject: usually 2–4 plain words, specific to context or problem, lowercase when natural. Avoid recipient first name, urgency, emojis, percentages, sales claims, fake `Re:`/`Fwd:`, or product pitches. Use one normal greeting, a context-to-offer bridge, approved sender identity/proof, one or two matched items, and one calm invitation to reply. Do not ask for a long meeting in first touch. No attachment, image, or multiple-link suggestion unless user requests it.

Choose shape from evidence, not habit:

- verified observation, relevant implication, offer bridge, soft reply ask;
- targeted question, concise value, proof, soft reply ask;
- trigger, useful insight, offer bridge, soft reply ask;
- similar-customer problem, approved proof, relevance question.

Social DM: shorter and conversational; refer naturally to the public post or use case without describing research mechanics. Check that the platform permits a message or connection; show the exact profile route and the source post/comment separately. Mention the discovery platform at most once, only when useful. Call brief: company activity, fit, offer angle, evidence, number provenance, likely buyer desk, and opening question. For `explore`, ask whether the need or workflow applies; never assert proven fit. Before inviting anyone to trial a product, verify that access is available. If availability is unknown, ask for a reaction or feedback without promising access.

If follow-ups are requested, prepare up to four. Each must stand alone and add one new useful angle, proof point, insight, or resource. Never write “just checking in,” guilt the recipient, or repeat the first email. Final follow-up closes the loop respectfully. Stop-sequence and reply handling remain outside Aliasearch unless separately requested and authorized.

Default user-facing columns: `Prospect`, `Location/market`, `Best route`, `Why this fits`, `Draft`. Use `Company` or `Person/account` where clearer. Include `Subject` only for email and clickable profile/source links when useful. Put phone and email in Contact without repeating them elsewhere. Add fields only when needed for action. Keep held leads and internal gate details out of the default workbook; provide exploratory leads separately only if requested. For Excel, use a frozen header, filters, legible widths, wrapped text, accessible contrast, and functional links; verify the workbook opens and content is readable. If Excel creation fails, provide CSV and explain the fallback. Internal ledger retains source links, dates and decisions.

Before delivery, check: first line reflects recipient context; verified fact connects to the offer reason; removing the name alone does not erase personalization; sender claims are approved; one offer angle and one ask; next step matches user's reply goal; copy reads naturally aloud and on a phone; no invented fact; route is sourced. Across a batch, messages must differ for evidence-based reasons, not merge tags alone. Measure human reply, qualified reply, sample/meeting, quote, and order separately. Do not infer deliverability or promise a conversion rate. Sending, domain setup, tracking, sequences, and inbox integration require separate authorization.
